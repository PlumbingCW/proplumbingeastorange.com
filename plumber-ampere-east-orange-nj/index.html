Ampere is one of the neighborhoods named in East Orange's 2018 Master Plan. Wikipedia says its defining feature was the Ampere train station and that the area developed after the Crocker Wheeler Company plant was built nearby. The station stood at Ampere Plaza and Whitney Place, between Springdale and Fourth Avenues. NJ Transit service ended in April 1991, and the station building no longer stands.

JerseyDigs describes the area as a mix of 19th-century row houses, Victorian-era homes, brick warehouses and small shops, plus Bakery Village, apartments in the former Ward Baking headquarters. This page looks at the water side of a building in a mixed area like that. It covers who owns which piece of pipe, what the lead line program means for owners, and how to read a high water bill.

Which Ampere This Page Covers

This page is for the Ampere section of East Orange. Ampere Parkway continues across the line into Ampere North, a separate community in Bloomfield Township. If your building is on the Bloomfield side, the page for Bloomfield is the better fit.

No official boundary for Ampere in East Orange is published. If you are not sure which side of the line you are on, give your street address when you call and we will sort it out first.

Who Owns Which Part Of The Water Line

Citywide, the East Orange Water Commission (99 S Grove Street, 973-266-8869) runs the water system and collects the sanitary sewer. The line from the street to the curb box is the Commission's responsibility. Everything beyond the curb box belongs to the building owner.

That split matters when water is leaking between the street and the meter. A wet patch near the curb is not automatically the owner's problem, but a leak past the curb box is. A water line repair on the owner's side starts with finding which side of the curb box the leak sits on.

The Sewer Lateral Is The Owner's Too

Citywide, the sewer lateral from the building to the connection with the Commission main is the owner's responsibility. In a row house or a converted warehouse, one lateral may serve several apartments. A backup then affects everyone in the building, and the owner is the person to call.

The Commission's minutes also record that East Orange's sanitary sewer discharges into a combined sewer carried by Newark. The board ordered an inflow and infiltration study to look into flooding. We cannot say how that applies to a particular Ampere street, so ask the Commission about the main on yours.

Lead Service Lines And What Owners Should Know

Citywide, East Orange is replacing lead service lines in phases. A $19 million loan closed in 2026 for about 1,790 lines, one of four remaining phases of roughly 10,000 lines in all. The work is tied to the state's July 2031 deadline.

We do not know the pipe material at any Ampere address, and age alone does not tell you. A service line has to be checked on site, or confirmed with the Commission. If you are planning to change interior pipe, repiping is the time to ask what material is coming into the building and where it connects.

Reading A High Water Bill

A high bill in a building with several units is hard to read. A running toilet, a dripping fixture or a leak under a slab or behind a wall can each add up, and a tenant may not see any of it. In general, the first check is the meter. Close every fixture in the building, then see whether the meter dial still moves.

If it moves, water is going somewhere. That is when water meter and high bill leak detection helps, because it separates a leak in the building from simple heavy use. A moving meter with no visible water points toward leak detection in walls, floors or buried pipe. A call to (973) 488-5410 is a good way to talk through what the dial is doing.

Older Mixed Buildings In General

In older buildings in general, plumbing is often patched over many years by different people. A shutoff may be hard to find, and one repair may sit next to pipe of a different age and material. That is a general point, and not a claim about any Ampere building.

Citywide, about three quarters of homes are in multi-unit buildings and roughly two thirds of occupied homes are rented. Heat season in rentals runs Oct 1 to May 15, at least 68 F by day and 65 F at night. For permit questions on a specific job, call the Building Division at 973-266-5320. New Jersey law requires that plumbing work be done by a master plumber.
