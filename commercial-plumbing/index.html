Commercial Plumbing Emergency? Do This First

When a line fails in an occupied building, the first ten minutes decide how much of the building gets wet. These steps protect the property and the people in it while the crew is on the way.

Find and close the shutoff

Know where the main building shutoff sits and where each riser or zone valve is. Closing the nearest valve stops the loss without cutting water to every tenant.

Protect power and inventory

Water near panels, registers, coolers or stock is the costly part. Move product up off the floor and keep staff away from wet electrical equipment.

Stop the fixtures upstream

If a drain or grease line is backing up, stop dishwashing, mop sinks and restroom use on that line until the crew arrives.

Photograph and note the time

Take photos of the damage and write down when it started. Your insurer and the building owner will both ask for it.

Storefronts On Central Avenue And Main Street

The city's master plan names Main Street and Central Avenue as the commercial corridors, and describes a 1940 to 1950 building boom there that added high-rises and commercial space. Many ground floor shops sit beneath apartments, so a store's restroom line, its water heater and the stack above it are often tangled together. A leak that starts upstairs can show up as a stained ceiling in a shop.

Older buildings in the corridor also tend to carry older piping, including galvanized supply and cast iron waste. We open the access panels, trace the lines to the source and explain what is failing before we recommend a repair or a larger replacement through repiping. Small shops usually need a fast, contained fix and a clear invoice, and that is how we scope it.

Restaurants, Grease Lines And Kitchen Plumbing

Food service puts the hardest duty on a drain system. Grease cools in the line, hardens, and narrows the pipe until a three compartment sink or a floor drain overflows during service. We clear these lines by cable, and when the buildup is heavy we recommend hydro jetting so the pipe wall is scrubbed instead of just opened up.

We also repair prep sinks, pre-rinse sprayers, hand sinks, mop sinks, floor drains and the water heater that feeds the kitchen. If the same line keeps closing, a camera inspection shows whether grease alone is the cause or whether the pipe has sagged or cracked.

Grease Trap Cleaning

A grease trap or interceptor catches fat before it reaches the building drain, and it only works while it has room. Once the trap is full, grease passes through and the downstream line closes. Signs include slow floor drains, odor at the kitchen sink and a trap that shows a solid cap of fat when the lid comes off.

We clean the trap, check the baffles and inlet and outlet fittings, and tell you how fast it filled so you can set a realistic schedule. Local pumping and disposal requirements for a specific restaurant are something we have not verified, so call the East Orange Building Division at 973-266-5320 and confirm what your business must follow.

Multifamily Buildings: Risers, Stacks And Shared Lines

In a building with 20 or more units, one failed part affects many households. A cracked drain stack can wet the units below, and a failing riser valve can leave a whole line of apartments without water. We repair the failed part, test the surrounding pipe and report which other sections look weak.

Tenants should report problems to the owner or manager at once, and managers should call us with the unit numbers affected. We keep notes on each visit so the next call starts from what we already know. Our drain cleaning and toilet repair crews handle the routine stoppages that fill a typical building's work orders.

Commercial Leak Detection

Not every leak announces itself with a puddle. In a commercial building, water can run inside a wall, under a slab or above a ceiling for weeks while the water bill climbs and mold begins. Signs are a meter that moves when the building is closed, damp drywall, a hot spot on a floor, or a sound of running water in a quiet space.

We shut down sections to isolate where the loss is, then pinpoint it before opening any finished surface. The process and equipment are the same ones described on our leak detection page, scaled to larger systems with more branches. Once the source is found, we explain the repair options and what it will take to restore the finish.

Water Heaters And Boilers For Buildings

Hot water in a commercial or multifamily property comes from a larger tank, a bank of tanks, or a boiler loop. When it fails, tenants and customers notice within hours. We diagnose the unit, repair what can be repaired, and give an honest opinion when a replacement is the better use of the owner's money.

For heated buildings, New Jersey's rental housing rules set a heat season from October 1 to May 15 with minimums of 68 degrees by day and 65 degrees at night, according to N.J.A.C. 5:10-14.4. A boiler outage in that window needs fast action, so see our boiler repair page for how we approach it and water heater replacement for tank swaps.

Backflow Testing

A backflow preventer keeps water from a building's internal systems from flowing backward into the public supply. According to the state's DCA Bulletin 99-2, testable backflow preventers that isolate cross connections are tested before final inspection and then every year. The tester must be certified by an agency recognized by the state's drinking water bureau, and the owner arranges the test.

After a passing test, the municipal code office issues a Certificate of Compliance good for one year. The same bulletin says testable preventers on one and two family dwellings are not required to be tested annually. Rules for irrigation systems and for specific assembly types are not something we can state from the bulletin, so call the Building Division at 973-266-5320 to confirm your case.

After-Hours Response And Written Reports

Our emergency line is staffed around the clock, and a person answers it. An after-hours crew can stop a loss, make a safe temporary repair and leave the permanent work for business hours when that makes sense. We tell you which of those we are doing and why.

Property managers need documentation, so we write up each visit with the location, the cause, what was repaired and what was recommended but not done. Owners and insurers can read it without a phone call, and a tenant dispute is easier to settle when the record is clear. Ask us to send it to more than one address if several people need a copy.

What Commercial Plumbing Costs: The Pricing Factors

Commercial jobs vary too much for a phone quote. We explain the price before work starts, and these are the things that move it.

Size and complexity of the systemA single restroom line is a short job. A riser serving six floors, or a kitchen with many connected fixtures, takes more time and parts.
Access to the pipeA line in an open mechanical room is easier to reach than one behind a finished ceiling or under a slab.
Equipment and partsCommercial valves, heaters and backflow assemblies are larger and often special order.
Time and dayNights, weekends and holidays carry after-hours rates because a crew is held for them.
Permits and testingWork that alters piping needs a permit through the Building Division, and backflow tests involve a certified tester.
