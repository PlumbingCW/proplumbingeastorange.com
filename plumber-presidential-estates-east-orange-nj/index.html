Water heater choices, repiping decisions and sewer lateral checks for owners of larger houses in Presidential Estates, also called Presidential Heights.

Presidential Estates is a name you will see on maps, in real estate listings and in neighborhood write-ups for East Orange. It is not the only name. The city's 2018 Master Plan calls the same part of town Presidential Heights, so both names are in use, and a search for one will not always turn up the other.

Wikipedia says the area was designated because its streets are named after early US presidents, and describes it as known for large homes on streets lined with old shade trees. This page is for owners of bigger houses like that. It covers the plumbing decisions that tend to come up with them, and it stays general where the sources say nothing about a particular house.

One Neighborhood, Two Names

The Master Plan says Presidential Heights. Other sources, including Wikipedia and the Statistical Atlas, say Presidential Estates. The Statistical Atlas lists it next to Ampere and Greenwood. No official boundary is published that we could find, so we do not draw one here.

In practice this only matters when you are looking things up. If you are checking a deed, a listing or a city document, try both names. For plumbing work, the street address is what counts. Call (973) 488-5410 and ask about plumbing service at your address.

Water Heater Age And The Replace Decision

In general, a tank water heater is a machine with a limited life, and the age printed on its rating plate is the first thing to check. A tank that is well past its expected years and leaking at the base is usually finished. A tank that is older but dry may only need a part, such as a valve or a thermostat.

In a large house the unit often does more work than in a small one. More bathrooms, longer pipe runs and heavier use all load the tank. When a repair would cost a large share of a new unit, owners often decide it is time to replace. That is the line between water heater repair and water heater replacement, and we can talk through which side your unit is on.

Tankless Or Tank: What Changes

A tankless unit heats water as it flows, so it does not keep a tank of hot water waiting. A tank unit stores hot water and refills when it runs low. Neither is right for every house, and the choice depends on how the household uses hot water.

Tankless units need enough gas or electric capacity, proper venting and a place on the wall, and those details differ from one house to the next. We cannot judge them from the street. For a closer look at the option, see tankless water heater installation, or call to talk through your layout.

Whole-House Repiping: A Decision Made On Evidence

Repiping means replacing the supply pipes that carry water through the house. It is a large job, and it should rest on what is found inside the walls. We do not assume any pipe material from a house's age or neighborhood. The material in your house has to be checked on site.

Reasons owners consider it in general include repeated leaks at different spots, low flow at several fixtures, or discolored water that returns after flushing. A single leak does not by itself mean the whole house needs new pipe. If you are weighing it, repiping is the page to read, and a call can help sort out whether a repair or a full replacement fits.

Sewer Lateral Inspection Before You Buy Or After A Slow Drain

Citywide, the East Orange Water Commission collects the sanitary sewer, and the sewer lateral from the home to the connection with the Commission main is the owner's responsibility. For a buyer, that means the lateral is part of what comes with the house. For an owner, it means a slow main drain is your pipe to investigate.

In general, tree roots are one common cause of lateral trouble in any older neighborhood with mature trees, along with cracked or shifted pipe and built-up grease. We are not saying roots are a problem on any street here. A camera inspection is how you find out what is actually in the line.

What A Camera Inspection Shows

A small camera on a flexible cable runs through the lateral and sends video back. It can show the pipe material, a sag where water pools, a cracked or offset joint, root growth or a blockage.

A buyer can use the recording to decide on a house before closing. An owner with a slow drain can use it to choose between cleaning and repair. Read more at sewer inspection, and if the line only needs clearing, see drain cleaning.

Who Pulls The Permit

Citywide, ordinary maintenance work needs no permit under N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7. Plumbing work in New Jersey must be done by a master plumber under state law. Whether a particular replacement or repipe needs a permit is a question for the city, so confirm it with the Building Division at 973-266-5320 before work begins.

If you are buying, ask the seller whether permits were pulled for past water heater or pipe work. Those records, where they exist, are held by the city. We do not guess at what was filed for any house.
