Tankless Water Heater Rebates in East Orange, NJ: What To Confirm First

The federal credit is gone for 2026 installs and the PSE&G pages do not agree. Here is what to ask, what to keep and how to avoid missing the claim window.

Call any hour, day or night.

☎ Call Now

By the East Orange Pro Plumbing team · October 23, 2026..

If you are asking whether a rebate or tax credit will lower the cost of a tankless water heater in New Jersey, the answer has changed. The federal 25C credit ended. The IRS states that the credit is not allowed for property placed in service after December 31, 2025, so a unit installed in 2026 does not qualify.

What remains is utility money. PSE&G lists a tankless water heater rebate on its appliance pages, but those pages do not say whether the offer is currently funded, and two PSE&G pages give different amounts. Because of that we will not print a dollar figure here.

The safe plan is to call PSE&G at 1-855-846-2895, confirm the current status in writing and keep every receipt. The sections below explain what to ask and what to save.

What Happened To The Federal Tax Credit

The Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit, often called 25C, used to cover part of the cost of qualifying water heaters. The IRS has updated its guidance to say that no credit is allowed for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. Placed in service means installed and ready for use, not the date you signed a contract.

Some older web pages still describe a 30 percent credit with a fixed maximum. Those pages are out of date. If a contractor or a blog quotes the credit for a 2026 installation, ask them to show you the current IRS page.

What PSE&G Says, And What It Does Not

PSE&G publishes post-purchase appliance rebates for its customers. Its appliance page lists a tankless water heater among qualifying equipment, with a requirement that the model qualify. A second, older PSE&G page for tankless units describes the offer differently and sets an efficiency requirement.

Neither page states an end date, and neither says whether money is available today. The pages also say rebates must be claimed within 120 days of purchase. That deadline makes a quick call more valuable than any number we could print.

What To Ask PSE&G On The Phone

Treat the call as a short interview. You want an answer that you can rely on when the installation is finished, not a general reassurance. Have your account details and the model you are considering in front of you.

  • Is the tankless water heater rebate open and funded right now for my account?
  • What exact model or efficiency requirement must the unit meet?
  • Does the rebate apply to a replacement only, or to a new installation too?
  • Does the installer need to be registered or authorized with PSE&G?
  • Which documents must I submit, and by what date?
  • Can I get the answers by email so I have a record?

Keep The Paperwork That Proves Your Claim

Claims fail more often from missing paper than from ineligible equipment. Keep the invoice that shows the model number, the date of purchase and the date of installation. Photograph the rating plate once the unit is mounted, and save the manufacturer's warranty card.

Because PSE&G's pages set a claim window of 120 days from purchase, write the deadline on a calendar the day you buy. If the installer can submit paperwork for you, ask what they will submit and request copies.

Is A Tankless Heater The Right Choice Without A Rebate?

A rebate should never be the reason to choose equipment. A tankless unit heats water on demand, so it does not store hot water, and its gas line, venting and water connections are a different design from a tank. Many older homes need the gas line sized up or the vent reworked.

Our tankless water heater page explains how we size and install these units, and our water heater installation team can compare a tankless unit with a new tank for your building. If you are replacing a failed tank, water heater replacement explains the steps from removal to inspection.

Tankless Or Tank: Questions To Settle Before You Chase A Rebate

Start with the household, not the offer. Count the bathrooms, the people and the times of day when hot water is drawn at once. A tankless unit has a maximum flow rate, and two showers plus a dishwasher running together can exceed what a small unit delivers.

Next, look at the space and the fuel. A gas tankless unit needs a vent that suits its exhaust, a source of combustion air and a gas line large enough for its input. If the old tank vented into a chimney, the new vent may need a different route, and that is often the largest part of the work.

Finally, think about the building. One unit for several apartments and one unit per apartment are very different projects. We explain the options after a visit, and you can call (973) 488-5410 to schedule one.

Remember too that a utility rebate is paid after purchase and installation. You carry the full cost first, so plan the budget on the whole price and treat any rebate as a later reduction, not a discount at the register.

What Affects Your Net Cost

A rebate is only one line in the total. These factors change what a tankless project costs before any rebate is applied.

Gas line and ventingA bigger gas line or a new vent route adds work and materials.
Unit capacityA larger household or several bathrooms calls for a larger unit.
Existing locationA utility closet needs less work than moving the heater to a new wall.
Water qualityVery hard water shortens the time between descaling services.
Permits and inspectionA permit and inspection are part of the job when the code requires them.

What This Means In East Orange

The city's own water page describes East Orange water as very hard, and the Water Commission draws groundwater from 18 wells in the Passaic aquifer. Hard water leaves scale on the heat exchanger of any water heater, tankless units included. Plan for periodic flushing, and ask the installer about isolation valves that make flushing easier.

Housing here is mostly multifamily. The city's draft housing element reports 14.1 percent of units in two-unit buildings and 11.9 percent in buildings of three or four units, and 69 percent of occupied homes are renter occupied. A landlord of a small building should ask PSE&G whether the offer covers that building type.

For permits, call the East Orange Building Division at 973-266-5320. Replacing a water heater is normally permitted work, and we confirm the requirement with the city for each job. We also serve nearby towns, including West Orange and Irvington, and you can call (973) 488-5410 to plan a tankless project.
