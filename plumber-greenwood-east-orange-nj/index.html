Greenwood is a small part of East Orange with two names. The city's 2018 Master Plan lists it as Greenwood and adds "Teen Streets" in parentheses. Wikipedia says the name comes from Greenwood Avenue and the numbered "teen" streets, so you will see both names in print.

This page is about drains, sewer laterals and basement water, since those are the plumbing problems that do not stay behind one wall. Call (973) 488-5410 to talk through what your drain or basement is doing.

Two Names And A Fuzzy Boundary

No official boundary for Greenwood is published, so we do not draw one here. Wikipedia describes the area as heavily disrupted by construction of Interstate 280 and the Garden State Parkway, and it is often grouped with Ampere. Ampere, Presidential Estates and Doddtown are the neighboring areas named in our sources.

City-Data is an aggregator, and its figures are estimates, not official counts. It lists streets in the area including North 15th to North 19th Streets, Park Avenue, Eaton Place, William Street and Greenwood Avenue. It puts the area at about 0.15 square miles, with ZIP 07017 and both East Orange and Newark school districts. That last detail tells you the area touches the Newark line.

What The Aggregator Figures Suggest About Shared Drains

City-Data estimates that about 27.5 percent of homes in the area are detached houses. If that is close, most units here sit in buildings with more than one household. It also estimates that over a third of units were built in 1939 or earlier, and most before 1960. Citywide, about three quarters of East Orange homes are in multi-unit buildings, according to the American Community Survey.

In multi-unit buildings in general, several apartments often share one drain line to the street. A backup can then show up at the lowest fixture, which may be a basement floor drain or a first-floor tub. A building's age says nothing by itself about what the pipes are made of. Pipe material has to be checked on site, house by house.

When A Drain Backs Up: What To Check First

Start by counting how many fixtures are affected. One slow sink usually points to a local clog, and drain cleaning often clears it. If the tub gurgles when the washer drains, or a basement floor drain bubbles, the problem is likely farther down the line.

  • Run water in a second fixture and see whether it backs up too.
  • Stop laundry, dishwashers and long showers until you know where the water is going.
  • Look at the basement floor drain for standing water or a smell.
  • Ask neighbors or other units in the building whether they see the same thing.
  • Note when it started and whether it followed heavy rain.

When the whole building is affected, the cause may be in the lateral or beyond it. Sewer line cleaning can open a stoppage, and sewer inspection with a camera shows what is in the pipe. Call (973) 488-5410 and describe the pattern you are seeing.

The Sewer Lateral And The Combined Sewer Citywide

Citywide, the East Orange Water Commission runs the water system and collects the sanitary sewer. Its office is at 99 S Grove Street, 973-266-8869. The sewer lateral from the home to the connection with the Commission main is the owner's responsibility. The line from the street to the curb box is the Commission's, and beyond the curb box is the owner's.

The Commission's minutes record that East Orange's sanitary sewer discharges into a combined sewer carried by Newark. The board ordered an inflow and infiltration study for flooding. This is a citywide matter, and we do not claim it explains any one address in Greenwood. Readers across the line can see our Newark page.

Basement Water After Big Storms

The Master Plan says that after Superstorm Sandy in 2012, East Orange had citywide power outages and may see basement flooding throughout the city. That is a citywide statement, not a flood map of Greenwood. Where water comes in and why is a question for each basement.

Water can enter a basement through a floor drain, through a crack, or from the ground outside. In general, a sump pump moves water out of a pit, but it needs power and a clear discharge line to work. If you already have a pit, sump pump service can check the pump and the line. If the water keeps returning, basement waterproofing is a separate conversation about the structure.

Permits And Who Does The Work

Ordinary maintenance work needs no permit under N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7. Replacing sewer or drain piping is different, and state law sets who may do plumbing work. For the permit on a specific job, confirm with the East Orange Building Division at 973-266-5320.

If you rent, tell your landlord or building manager about a backup right away. Shared lines are usually the owner's concern. Call (973) 488-5410 if you own the building and want to talk through the problem.
