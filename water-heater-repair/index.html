A water heater fails in one of two ways. It stops making hot water, which is an inconvenience, or it leaks, which is a clock. In East Orange, where about 69 percent of homes are renter occupied and 38 percent of units sit in buildings of 20 or more apartments, one tank in a basement often serves several households. When it fails, the effects reach well beyond one kitchen.

We repair gas, electric and hybrid heat pump water heaters at any hour. The first question on the phone is always the same: is water on the floor? If it is, we start with shutoff steps and a dispatch plan. If it is only cold showers, we walk through a short set of checks and plan the visit around the likely part.

Our aim is an honest repair or replace answer on site. Some failures cost less than a service call to fix, and some mean the tank has reached the end of its life. We tell you which one you have before any work starts.

Water Heater Leaking? Shut It Down In This Order

A leak from the tank body is the most urgent water heater problem, because a corroded tank does not heal and can release its full volume. Before you call, take these steps if you can reach the unit safely. If you smell gas at any point, skip all of this, leave the building and follow the gas rule below.

Cut the fuel or power

For an electric unit, switch off its breaker. For a gas unit, turn the gas control knob to off or close the gas valve on the supply line.

Close the cold water inlet

Turn the valve on the cold pipe above the tank clockwise until it stops. This ends the refill that feeds the leak.

Protect the floor and the room

Lift boxes, rugs and stored items off the wet area. In a multifamily basement, tell neighbors whose storage cages sit nearby.

Smell gas? Leave first

Do not touch switches or phones inside. Move at least 350 feet away, then call PSE&G at 1-800-880-7734 or 911.

Once the unit is off and the water is isolated, call (973) 488-5410 and tell us the tank size, the fuel and where the water is coming from. A hot water tank can still scald after the power is off, so do not open the drain valve or touch the pipes until we advise.

Where Water Heater Leaks Actually Come From

Not every puddle means a dead tank. Leaks at the top usually come from the inlet and outlet fittings, the dielectric nipples or the relief valve, and those are often repairable. A drip from the temperature and pressure valve discharge pipe can mean the valve is worn, or that pressure in the system is climbing above where it should.

Leaks at the bottom are harder to read. A loose drain valve or a failed heating element gasket on an electric unit can be fixed. Water seeping from the base of a gas tank, especially with rust streaks or a hiss at the burner, usually points to a hole in the steel liner, and that tank cannot be patched.

We trace the water to its source before we quote anything. A wet floor can also come from a nearby pipe, a condensate line or a washing machine hose, so we look at the surroundings, not just the tank.

Pilot Light, Thermocouple And Gas Valve Problems

On a gas tank, the pilot flame heats a small sensor called a thermocouple. When that sensor weakens, it stops telling the gas valve that a flame is present, and the valve shuts off for safety. The symptom is a pilot that lights while you hold the button and then dies when you let go.

A new thermocouple is a small part, but the same symptom can also come from a dirty pilot tube or a worn gas control valve. We test before we replace. Gas work on a heater should be done by a licensed plumber, so we do not recommend repeated relighting attempts yourself if the flame will not stay on.

Draft and venting matter too. A downdraft from a blocked flue or a closed basement room starved of air can blow a pilot out. If we see soot, scorch marks or a flue that does not draw, we stop and fix the venting before the heater runs again.

Electric Elements, Thermostats And Hybrid Units

A standard electric tank has two heating elements and two thermostats. When the lower element burns out, you get a small amount of hot water and then lukewarm. When the upper one fails, you get nothing at all. A meter reading tells us which one is bad in a few minutes.

Hybrid heat pump models add a compressor, a fan and a condensate drain, which means more things that can raise an error code. Many of those problems are a blocked filter or a clogged drain line, not a failed compressor. We read the code, check the basics and tell you if the fault is a part we can replace or a manufacturer service item.

Hard Water Scale, Sediment And Anode Rods In East Orange

The city itself describes East Orange water as very hard, and its softening page notes scale buildup in water heaters. Minerals settle out when water is heated, and they pile up on the tank bottom and coat the elements. That layer makes burners work harder, causes popping and rumbling, and shortens element life.

The anode rod is the sacrificial metal rod inside the tank that corrodes so the steel does not. Once it is used up, the tank starts rusting from the inside. Checking or replacing the rod and flushing the tank are the two maintenance jobs that most often add years to a heater, and they are worth doing before a leak starts.

Repair Or Replace: How We Decide

Age is the first input, but not the only one. A tank near the end of its rated life that leaks from the body should be replaced, and a newer tank with a failed thermostat should be repaired. In between, we weigh the part cost, the condition of the tank, the venting and your plans for the building.

If we recommend replacement, we explain why and show you what we found. Our water heater replacement page covers how same-day swaps work. If you are considering a different type of unit, see tankless water heaters for when they make sense and when they do not.
