A drain that will not clear is rarely just a nuisance in East Orange. Most of the housing here is multifamily, and the city's own housing plan counts about 69 percent of homes as renter occupied and 38 percent of units in buildings with 20 or more apartments. When one line stops, the water often shows up in a neighbor's tub or a basement floor drain. We clear kitchen, bathroom, laundry and main drains at any hour, and we explain the cause before we recommend a method.

Call the 24/7 line and describe what the water is doing. A technician will ask which fixtures are affected, because that tells us whether the blockage sits in a single trap or in the building line. If it is a single fixture, a cable machine usually solves it in one visit. If several fixtures back up together, we plan for the main line from the start.

Water Backing Up Right Now? Do This First

Slow draining and gurgling can wait for a daytime visit. Water coming up through a floor drain, a toilet that fills the tub when you flush, or a kitchen sink that rises when the washer drains is different. Those are signs of a blockage past the fixture, and every minute of use adds more water to the floor.

Stop adding water

No flushing, showers, laundry or dishwasher cycles until the line is cleared. Tell other units in the building to do the same.

Protect what is on the floor

Move rugs, boxes and electronics off a wet basement floor. Keep children and pets out of standing water that came from a sewer line.

Skip the chemical drain opener

Caustic products can sit in a stopped line and burn skin when a technician opens the pipe. They also stress old cast iron joints.

Call and describe the symptoms

Tell us which drains are slow, which are backing up and whether the building has other apartments on the same stack.

What Actually Blocks Drains In East Orange Buildings

Age is the first factor. The city's draft housing element reports that 29.8 percent of occupied homes were built in 1939 or earlier, and the median home dates to 1957. Pipes from that period are often cast iron or clay, and the inside surface roughens over decades. Grease, hair and paper catch on that roughness and build up into a narrow channel that finally closes.

Water chemistry is the second. The city's own hard water page describes East Orange water as very hard. Hard water leaves mineral scale on pipe walls and on the elbows under sinks, and scale gives every other kind of debris a place to grab. This is why a drain can clear for a few weeks and then slow down again.

Building use is the third. A kitchen stack serving four or six apartments carries cooking grease from every unit, and a laundry line collects lint and soap residue. Roots matter less in dense blocks than they do in West Orange or Bloomfield, but older clay laterals under front yards can still be invaded at the joints.

Which Method We Use, And Why

We match the tool to the stoppage instead of running the same machine on every call. A small drum machine handles sink and tub lines. A larger sectional cable handles a main line. When a cable only punches a hole in the buildup, we recommend hydro jetting so the pipe wall is scrubbed clean, and when the same drain keeps clogging we recommend a camera inspection to see the real condition of the pipe.

One Fixture Or The Whole Line? How To Tell

If one sink or one tub is slow and every other drain works, the problem is almost always in that fixture's trap or branch line. Hair in a bathroom tub and grease in a kitchen sink account for most of these calls. They are quick and inexpensive to clear.

If the first floor toilet gurgles when the upstairs shower runs, or a basement floor drain bubbles after laundry, the main line is the likelier cause. Those calls usually involve sewer line cleaning and sometimes a sewer line repair if the pipe has collapsed or separated.

Who Is Responsible For A Blocked Line?

In East Orange the owner is responsible for the sewer lateral from the building to the point where it joins the Water Commission main, according to the commission's own program documents. The part in the street belongs to the utility. If a backup is caused by the public main, the Water Commission at 973-266-8869 is the office to notify, and we will tell you on site if what we find points that way.

Tenants should report a backup to the landlord right away and keep a record of the time and the affected fixtures. We write up what we found and what we did so a landlord, property manager or insurer can see the cause. Clearing stoppages is ordinary maintenance under New Jersey's construction code and does not by itself need a permit, but replacing drain piping does, so a repair that changes pipe is a different conversation.

Keeping The Line Open After We Leave

Collect kitchen grease in a can and wipe pans before washing. Put a hair catcher in every tub and shower. Flush toilet paper only, and keep wipes labeled flushable out of the line, since they do not break down the way paper does.

In multifamily buildings, a scheduled cleaning of the main line is often cheaper than repeated emergency calls. If you manage a building, ask us about a maintenance cleaning and see our commercial plumbing page for how we handle multi-unit properties.
