Is a plumber coming right now? Yes. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for Bloomfield, which borders East Orange to the north along about 2.7 kilometers of shared line. Our shop at 524 Central Avenue is a short drive from the Watsessing Park edge of town.

Bloomfield has its own mix of old two-family houses and a water system with a story worth knowing. This page works through the questions Bloomfield owners and tenants ask us most, starting with who pays for the lead line work now under way.

Who Pays For Lead Service Line Work In Bloomfield?

Bloomfield's 2024 water quality report says the township has about 11,704 service connections and estimates that about a third of the service lines may contain lead. It reports more than 3,400 lines inspected and about 1,300 lead or galvanized lines replaced, and it states that replacement is at no cost to property owners. The same report says the township code puts the meter-to-curb-box segment on the owner.

That looks contradictory, so ask the Water Department which rule applies to your line before you pay anyone. A lead service line inventory is posted on the township website. If a crew has already replaced the township side, we can make the connection and fix the house piping, and our water line repair visit starts with a look at what material you actually have.

What Should I Do If A Pipe Bursts In A Bloomfield Two-Family?

About 49.3 percent of Bloomfield homes are renter occupied and 54.6 percent sit in multi-unit buildings, according to the 2024 American Community Survey. The median home was built in 1950. In a two-family the upstairs supply line leaks onto the downstairs ceiling, and owners and tenants both need to move fast.

Shut the main, relieve pressure at a low faucet and call. We find the split with our leak detection tools if it is hidden, and we repair only the damaged section when the surrounding pipe is sound. Tenants should tell the owner in writing and keep a photo record.

Why Does Bloomfield Water Come Through Newark's System?

The township buys bulk water from the North Jersey District Water Supply Commission, which draws from the Wanaque reservoirs. Bloomfield cannot take that water directly, so it is wheeled through Newark's Pequannock system, according to the 2024 report. All of the drinking water originates from the Pequannock watershed.

For you, this means a main problem in the street goes to the township, while the pipe inside your property is ours to deal with. We do not make claims about the mineral content of your tap water, because the report does not give a Bloomfield-specific figure. If scale on fixtures worries you, we can look at it on site.

Before We Arrive If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock. These four steps cut the damage while the plumber is on the way. Half of Bloomfield homes are rented, so some of them involve the landlord.

Close the main valve

Turn it clockwise until it stops. In a two-family the shutoff may be in the basement the owner controls, so call them right away. If the valve is stuck, do not force it, and tell us.

Kill power near water

Switch off breakers for wet rooms only from dry floor. If water is around the panel or flowing near a basement outlet, stay clear and call 911.

Clear the area and tell the other unit

Move belongings off the floor, tell the neighbor above or below you and photograph the damage with the date. Tenants should notify the owner in writing so repairs and insurance are documented.

Call our 24/7 line

Call (973) 488-5410 and tell us the floor, the symptom and whether the main is off. We dispatch an emergency plumber and explain the price before work starts.

Which Services Do Bloomfield Emergencies Usually Need?

The mix here leans toward supply and drain problems. Hidden leaks and service line trouble lead the list, and the lead replacement program means more owners are looking at old pipe than usual. A backup that runs through the basement floor drain calls for drain cleaning, and a burst in a wall needs burst pipe repair first and cosmetic work later.

Bloomfield's report also notes aging pipes and valves in the public system and describes a multi-million investment in main cleaning, lining and hydrant and valve replacement. Street work can cause short outages or air in the lines after water returns. Run a cold tap for a minute before you assume the pipe inside your home is damaged.

What Local Facts Should Bloomfield Owners Know?

The township's Construction Department is at 1 Municipal Plaza, First Floor, Room 105, phone 973-680-4053, and it issues permits and inspections under the state Uniform Construction Code. Under N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7, clearing a stoppage is ordinary maintenance and needs no permit, while replacing piping does and the permit is signed by a licensed master plumber. Call before a larger job so you know what to expect.

The township municipal building is at 1 Municipal Plaza and the main line is 973-680-4000. For a street-side issue, ask for the Water Department and tell them the cross street and what you see, such as a main break or low pressure.

Watsessing Park is shared with East Orange and includes sections of the Second River and Toney's Brook. East Orange's own master plan records flooding along the Second River, so basements near the park deserve a floor drain and sump check before heavy rain. Brookdale Park, the Garden State Parkway interchanges at exits 148 to 151 and the Bloomfield NJ Transit station are good reference points when you call.

Bloomfield College of Montclair State University, near the green, and the Oakes Estate are named landmarks. If a leak affects a commercial storefront downtown, tell us the cross street and whether it opens to the public, and see our commercial plumbing page for how we handle those jobs.
