A Plumber Built Around East Orange Buildings

East Orange is a city of apartment buildings and older houses. The city's draft housing plan counts about 69 percent of homes as renter occupied, and 29.8 percent of occupied homes were built in 1939 or earlier. That mix shapes almost every call we take. A clog in a six unit building affects neighbors. A galvanized supply line from the 1950s fails in a different way than a copper line from the 1990s.

We work from 524 Central Avenue, so a crew is a short drive from most addresses in the city and from nearby Orange, Newark, Bloomfield, West Orange and Irvington. The line is answered 24 hours a day by a person who asks what the water is doing, and we tell you what to shut off while the crew is on the way.

What Local Water And Weather Do To Plumbing

The East Orange Water Commission draws water from 18 wells in the Passaic aquifer, and the city's own hard water page calls it very hard. Mineral scale builds up inside water heaters, aerators, toilet fill valves and old pipes, which is why we flush tanks and check scale on nearly every water heater visit. Our water heater pages explain when [[water-heater-repair|repair]] makes sense and when [[water-heater-replacement|replacement]] is the better call.

Winter adds a second set of problems. Newark averages about 80 days a year at or below freezing, and New Jersey's frost depth in this part of the state is 36 inches. Exposed supply lines in unheated basements and exterior walls are the usual casualties, and [[burst-pipe-repair|burst pipe repair]] is a standing part of our January schedule. Heavy rain is the third: a storm like Hurricane Ida put more than 3 inches of rain on Newark in a single hour, and storms of that kind push water into basements and overwhelm drains.

When To Call The Emergency Line

Call right away for a burst or leaking pipe, sewage coming up from a floor drain, a water heater leaking onto the floor, no heat or hot water in an occupied building, or a gas smell. For gas, leave the building first and call PSE&G at 1-800-880-7734 or 911 from a safe distance, then call us for the plumbing side. For everything else, the same number works during the day and we will book the earliest visit we can.

Water everywhere

Shut the main valve, switch off power to wet areas and call us. See burst pipe repair for the full list.

Drain backing up

Stop using water in the building and keep the floor clear. Our drain cleaning team will ask which fixtures are affected.

No hot water

Check the breaker or pilot, then call if it will not restart. Landlords have heat season duties from October 1 to May 15.

Mystery wet spot

A damp ceiling or a high water bill points to a hidden leak. Start with leak detection before opening walls.

How We Price Plumbing Work

We do not quote numbers for problems we have not seen. On site, we diagnose the cause, explain the options in plain words and give you a price before work starts. What moves a price: the type and location of the problem, how easy it is to reach, the pipe or equipment involved, whether the call is after hours, and any permit work that applies. Where a permit is required, the work follows New Jersey's plumbing subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15), which the state lists as the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2024 New Jersey edition. If a better fix costs more, we tell you why, and if the cheaper fix is enough, we tell you that too.
