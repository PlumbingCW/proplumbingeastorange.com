How Long Does Sump Pump Installation Take In East Orange, NJ?

A swap in an existing pit is quick. A new pit through the slab is a bigger job. Here is what sets the schedule and what to confirm with the city.

Call any hour, day or night.
(973) 488-5410

By the East Orange Pro Plumbing team · October 23, 2026..

Replacing a sump pump in an existing pit often takes a single visit measured in hours, while installing a new pit through the basement slab usually takes longer, often a day or more and sometimes extra time for the concrete patch to cure. The biggest factor is whether a pit already exists. Electrical work, the check valve and the discharge route add to the time.

Two Very Different Jobs

JobWhat happensWhy it takes the time it does
Replace a pump in an existing pit.Disconnect the old pump, set the new pump, fit a check valve, reconnect the discharge, test.Mostly a quick swap, with extra time if pipes or fittings are corroded.

What Takes Time In A New Pit

A new pit starts with locating where water collects, which may be along a wall or at a low corner. The crew cuts the slab, breaks it out, removes soil and rock, and sets a basin with a gravel bed. Digging a pit in older New Jersey basements can involve fill, old drain lines or rock, and each one takes time.

After the pump is in, the crew patches the slab around the basin. Concrete patches usually need time to set and cure before heavy use, and that is part of the schedule. We explain the plan before we start, so you know when the area can be used again.

Electrical: Where Schedules Slip

A sump pump needs power at the pit, and a standard pump should not share an overloaded circuit. If there is no suitable outlet, one may need to be added. Electrical work can need a licensed electrician and its own permit, so confirm with the East Orange Building Division at 973-266-5320.

Power also matters in storms. Sandy knocked out power citywide in East Orange, according to the city's 2018 Master Plan, and a pump on household power stops when the lights go out. A battery backup is worth a conversation, and it adds a bit of time on installation.

The Check Valve And Discharge Line

A check valve sits on the discharge pipe and stops water from running back into the pit after the pump shuts off. Without one, the pump cycles more often and wears out faster. Fitting the valve and checking for leaks is a small step, but it must be done carefully.

The discharge pipe carries the water out of the house. Where it goes is the question that can change the schedule the most. A short run to the outside takes little time. A long run, a run that crosses a wall, or a run that needs digging takes much longer. See our sump pump page for how we plan these runs.

What This Means In East Orange

We did not find a verified East Orange or New Jersey rule on where sump pump discharge may go, so we do not state one. Confirm discharge rules with the East Orange Building Division at 973-266-5320 before any plumbing work. Never connect a sump pump discharge to the sanitary sewer unless the city confirms it is allowed, because stormwater can overload the sewer and cause backups.

On flooding history, the city's 2018 Master Plan says East Orange was affected by Superstorm Sandy, that the city may experience basement flooding throughout, and that flooding occurs along the Second River and Nishuane Brook. Hurricane Ida in September 2021 broke Newark's rainfall record, with more than 3 inches in one hour, according to NBC News. A heavy burst like that can overwhelm drains and push water into basements.

The Water Commission minutes of May 21, 2024 record the Director saying the city discharges its sanitary sewer into a combined sewer carried by Newark. That is another reason to keep clean stormwater out of the sanitary line. Call 811 before digging outside, because New Jersey requires notice at least three business days and no more than 10 business days before excavation.

Freezing And The Outside Discharge

The frost depth for Essex County in the state's one- and two-family code table is 36 inches. A discharge line that is shallow or exposed can freeze in winter and trap water in the pit. Newark's climate normals show about 79.9 days a year at or below 32 F.

That does not mean every outdoor discharge is a problem. It means the route and slope should be planned so water drains out of the pipe after each cycle. We look at the freeze risk before we lay out the discharge.

Preparing For The Visit

A little preparation shortens the visit and avoids surprises.

  • Clear a path from the door to the basement and a working area around the pit or the low spot.
  • Move stored items away from the wall and the floor near the pit.
  • Note where water enters and how high it gets in a storm. Photos help.
  • Find the electrical panel and any outlets near the pit.
  • Tell us if the home is in a flood-prone area and whether the pump ever runs dry.

DIY Versus Calling A Pro: Honest Limits

Replacing a pump in an existing pit is within reach for some handy owners. Shut off power at the breaker, disconnect the old pump, and reverse the steps with the new one. But a wrong check valve, a bad seal or a discharge line aimed at the wrong place can cause a flood or a violation.

Cutting a slab, setting a basin and running a discharge line are jobs for a plumber. In New Jersey, plumbing work is done by licensed people, and the permit is signed by the licensee. Never work on a pump while standing in water, and never touch a plugged-in pump.

Pump Problems That Look Like An Install Problem

Before you replace a pump, make sure it is the pump. A float that is stuck against the pit wall, a switch that has failed, or a clogged discharge can make a good pump look dead. A short check can save a replacement.

A pit that fills very fast may point to a bigger water problem. Our basement waterproofing page explains how drainage and sealing work together with a pump. If you suspect a leaking pipe rather than groundwater, leak detection can tell the two apart.
