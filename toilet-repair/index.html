A toilet that will not stop running wastes water all day, and one that overflows can ruin a floor in minutes. In East Orange, where most homes are rented and many sit above another unit, a toilet problem is rarely only your problem. We repair and install toilets at any hour and explain the cause before we recommend a fix.

Most toilet calls fall into a handful of causes. A clog sits in the bowl or trap, a flapper or fill valve inside the tank has worn out, a wax ring under the bowl has failed, or the toilet itself is cracked or loose. A short description over the phone usually tells us which one to expect.

Toilet Overflowing? Do This First

A rising bowl is an emergency in the moment, but it is usually stoppable in under a minute if you know where to reach.

Do not flush again

A second flush adds more water to a bowl that has nowhere to send it. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down if water is still running in.

Close the shutoff valve

The shutoff valve is on the wall or floor behind the toilet, near the base. Turn it clockwise until it stops and the tank stops refilling.

Protect the floor

Lay down towels and move rugs away. Sewage water is not clean, so keep children and pets out of it and wear gloves to clean up.

Check the other drains

If the tub or sink gurgles when you flush, the blockage may be past the toilet. Tell us, and tell neighbors in the building to hold off using water.

Running Toilets And Worn Tank Parts

A toilet that runs on its own, hisses, or refills every few minutes has a leaking flapper, a worn fill valve or a poorly set float. Water slips past the flapper into the bowl, the tank drops, and the fill valve opens to top it off. The meter keeps turning even though nobody has flushed.

To check a flapper, add a little food coloring to the tank and wait ten minutes without flushing. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper is leaking. Replacing a flapper, fill valve or flush handle is a short repair, and replacing a ball cock is listed as ordinary maintenance in N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7.

Hard Water Buildup Inside The Tank

The city describes East Orange water as very hard. Hard water leaves mineral crust on flappers, flush valve seats, fill valve screens and the small holes under the bowl rim. A flapper that no longer seals against a crusty seat leaks no matter how new the rubber is.

When we replace tank parts, we clean the seat and the fill valve inlet, and we tell you what we found. In older tanks the buildup is heavy enough that replacing the whole fill and flush valve assembly is cheaper over time than patching one worn part.

Clogs: Plunger, Auger Or Something Deeper

A single clogged toilet is usually a paper or wipe jam in the trap, and a toilet auger clears it without scratching the porcelain. If the toilet clogs again within days, or other drains in the unit gurgle at the same time, the blockage is likely in the branch or main line. That is a different job.

In that case we move to drain cleaning with a larger machine and, if the pattern repeats, a camera inspection to see what the pipe looks like. Wipes sold as flushable are one of the most common causes in multifamily buildings, because they do not break up the way paper does.

Wobbling Toilets, Wax Rings And Leaks At The Base

A toilet that rocks, or water around the base after a flush, points to a failed wax ring or loose closet bolts. The wax ring is the seal between the bowl and the drain flange, and it only works while the bowl is held firmly in place. A leak there can run under flooring for weeks before a stain shows downstairs.

We pull the toilet, inspect the flange and subfloor, replace the wax ring and bolts and reset the bowl level. If the flange is broken or the floor has soft spots, we tell you before we set the toilet again, because resetting on damaged wood just fails a second time.

Installing A New Toilet

Replacement makes sense when the bowl is cracked, the toilet is chronically clogging, or the old model wastes water. We remove the old unit, check the flange, set the new toilet with a fresh seal, connect a new supply line and test several flushes. Bring the model you want, or ask us what fits your rough-in distance and space.

Under N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7, like for like replacement of a fixture in a single family dwelling is treated as ordinary maintenance when no change is made to the piping arrangement. Moving a toilet, or changing the drain or supply layout, is a different category and needs a permit through the Building Division at 973-266-5320. Quick Checks Before You Call

A few checks take two minutes and help us arrive with the right parts. Lift the tank lid and see whether the water level sits about an inch below the overflow tube. Jiggle the handle and look for a chain that is too long or caught under the flapper.

Listen at the bowl after a flush. A trickle that continues for minutes points to the flapper, while a hiss that stops and starts points to the fill valve. Look at the supply line and shutoff for drips, and look at the floor around the base for dampness. Tell us what you found when you call, and we will plan the visit around it.

If a toilet is cracked in the bowl or tank, do not keep using it. A hairline crack can open under weight and release the whole tank at once. Close the shutoff and call, and we can set a replacement the same day when one is available.

Toilet Problems In Rentals And Apartment Buildings

About 69 percent of occupied homes in the city are renter occupied, so many toilet calls start with a tenant and end with a landlord's approval. Tenants should report the problem in writing as soon as it starts, with the time and what the fixture is doing. A small leak reported early costs less than a ceiling below it.

Landlords and managers can call us directly and ask for a written note on what failed and whether it was misuse or wear. In a building where several units share one stack, repeated toilet clogs often come from the shared line, and the fix belongs to the owner. We explain which it is on site.
