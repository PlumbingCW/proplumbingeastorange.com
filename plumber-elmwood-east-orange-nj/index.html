Plumber in Elmwood, East Orange, NJ

Boilers, shared water heaters, toilets and faucets in the small apartment buildings of Elmwood, and how to describe a problem clearly on a call.

Elmwood is a part of southeastern East Orange where a lot of people share one building. A plumbing problem here often belongs to several households at once: the tenant who notices it, the landlord who pays for it, and the neighbor upstairs whose floor is the ceiling. This page is written for those people, and for owners of two to four unit buildings who need to explain a problem clearly on a phone call.

The city's Master Plan names Elmwood as a neighborhood, but no official boundary is published, so we do not draw one. Call (973) 488-5410 to talk through the problem.

What The Numbers Say About Small Buildings Here

NeighborhoodScout is a commercial profile built on Census tract data, and it gives no year for its figures, so read them as a rough picture. It reports that Elmwood North is mostly small apartment buildings, with 37.8% of homes in buildings of 2 to 4 units and mostly renter occupied. For Elmwood West it reports 42.7% of homes in 2 to 4 unit buildings and 57.5% of homes built before 1940.

Citywide, East Orange is roughly two thirds renter occupied, and about three quarters of homes are in multi-unit buildings (ACS). Those are citywide figures, not Elmwood counts. What they suggest in general is that many plumbing calls in this city come from shared systems, where one boiler or one water heater serves more than one household.

Heat In A Rental: What The Rule Says

For rentals in New Jersey, the heat season runs from October 1 to May 15. During that season the rule (N.J.A.C. 5:10-14.4) sets a minimum of 68 F during the day and 65 F at night. If you rent in East Orange, those are the numbers to hold up when a radiator stays cold.

That rule is about heat. We have not verified a specific hot water standard for this page, so for a hot water complaint, ask the Building Division how it applies to your building.

Who To Call First When The Heat Or Water Fails

For a tenant, the usual order is simple. Tell the landlord or building manager first, in writing if you can, and note the date and time. If nothing happens, the Building Division at City Hall is the next call, at 973-266-5320.

A tenant should not hire a plumber to open up a boiler the landlord owns. Plumbing work must be done by a master plumber under New Jersey law, and ordinary maintenance work needs no permit (N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7). For the permit rules on a specific job, confirm with the Building Division.

Boilers And Shared Water Heaters In 2 To 4 Unit Buildings

In older small buildings in general, one boiler often heats every unit, and one tank may supply hot water to all of them. When that equipment fails, every household feels it. A cold radiator in one unit may be trapped air or a closed valve, while cold radiators in every unit point at the boiler itself.

If the whole building is cold, describe it that way on the call. We can talk through boiler repair and tell you what we would want to know first. For hot water, the same logic applies: one unit with lukewarm water may have a mixing valve problem, while no hot water anywhere points to the shared tank.

A shared tank that leaks at the base is usually at the end of its life, and water heater replacement becomes the question. A tank that still holds water but struggles may only need water heater repair. We confirm which one on site, because it depends on the unit in front of us.

Toilets And Faucets In A Building Everyone Uses

Fixture calls in a small building look minor and then become a dispute. A running toilet wastes water on a bill the landlord may pay, and a dripping kitchen faucet is easy to put off for months. Both are usually a worn part rather than a big job.

If a toilet in one unit rocks, leaks at the base or will not stop running, see toilet repair. For a faucet that drips or sprays sideways, see faucet repair.

How An Owner Can Describe The Problem Clearly

A clear call saves time for everyone, and owners of two to four unit buildings can prepare in a few minutes. Use this list before you dial.

  • The full street address and which unit or floor is affected.
  • What you see or hear: no heat, a cold radiator, no hot water, a leak, a gurgle.
  • Whether it is one unit or the whole building.
  • When it started and whether it is getting worse.
  • Where the boiler or water heater is, and whether you can get to it.
  • Whether the tenants have already contacted you, and whether the building is in the heat season.

That is the information we would ask for anyway. Then call (973) 488-5410 and ask about plumbing service at your address.

Elmwood Park And The Carnegie Branch For Orientation

Wikipedia describes Elmwood as in the southeastern part of the city, served by Elmwood Park, and says it has one of the surviving Carnegie libraries, a branch that opened in 1912. The Parks Master Plan puts Elmwood Park in the Third Ward at just under 9 acres.

Water And Sewer Responsibility, Citywide

The East Orange Water Commission (99 S Grove Street, 973-266-8869) runs the water and collects the sanitary sewer. Citywide, the line from the street to the curb box is the Commission's responsibility, and beyond the curb box is the owner's. The sewer lateral from the home to the connection with the Commission main is also the owner's.
